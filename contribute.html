02 / PRINCIPLES

What makes a useful contribution?

A

Be precise

State the definition, input, or mathematical convention involved. A precise description is easier to evaluate than a general observation.

B

Make it reproducible

For a suspected bug, provide the input and explain the expected result versus the observed result. Include the steps needed to reproduce the issue.

C

Separate computation from proof

Computed examples can provide evidence and help discover patterns, but they do not automatically establish a general mathematical statement.

D

Explain the reasoning

When suggesting a mathematical or algorithmic change, include the reasoning, relevant assumptions, and any known limitations.

03 / REPORTING AN ISSUE

What to include in a report

A useful report should make it possible to understand the problem without having to guess what happened.

  • — Location: the page, property, algorithm, or visualization involved.
  • — Input: the exact integer, definition, or sequence being examined.
  • — Expected result: what you believe should happen and why.
  • — Observed result: what actually happened, including any error message.
  • — Supporting reasoning: a proof, reference, derivation, or reproducible example where applicable.

04 / GETTING INVOLVED

The project is still taking shape.

Numtax is being developed incrementally, and its contribution workflow is not yet connected to a public submission channel. This page sets out the kinds of contributions that would be useful and the standards they should follow.

A public repository, issue tracker, or contact method can be linked here when an official channel is established.
